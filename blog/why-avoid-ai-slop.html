Why AI Slop Is Killing Your Business Website
(And What to Do Instead)

5 min read By Jiyoosh Karayil · June 2026

There's a version of using AI for your business website that works extraordinarily well. And there's a version that quietly destroys your credibility, suppresses your search rankings, and drives visitors away before they even read past the headline.

The second version has a name. The industry calls it AI slop — and it's everywhere right now. If you've been experimenting with AI writing tools and wondering why the needle isn't moving, this might be why.

What is AI Slop, Exactly?

AI slop isn't AI-generated content in general. It's the specific output you get when you prompt an AI tool without providing real context, genuine expertise, or editorial judgement.

It looks like this:

  • "In today's fast-paced digital landscape, businesses need to leverage cutting-edge solutions..."
  • "We are passionate about helping our clients achieve their goals through innovative strategies..."
  • "Are you looking to take your business to the next level? Our team of experts can help."

You've read hundreds of pages like this. They say everything and nothing simultaneously. They're grammatically correct, structurally sound, and completely empty. No specific insight. No real differentiation. No trust signal whatsoever.

The tell: AI slop is content that could have been written about any business in any industry, in any country, at any time. If you removed your company name and could replace it with any competitor's name without changing a word — you have AI slop.

Why It's Actively Damaging Your Business

Most business owners think the worst outcome of generic AI content is that it's boring. The real damage is significantly more concrete than that.

1. Google Now Penalises It Specifically

Google's Helpful Content System (rolled out from 2022 and strengthened in 2024) is specifically designed to identify and suppress content produced at scale that lacks genuine expertise or human-first perspective. The signals it looks for include:

  • Thin content that covers a topic without genuine depth
  • Content that doesn't match the claimed expertise of the site
  • Pages created primarily for search engines rather than human readers
  • Repetition of commonly-available information without original insight

If your website is filled with AI-generated copy that fits this profile, your entire domain pays the price — not just the individual pages. A content penalty can suppress your legitimate service pages, your homepage, even your Google Business Profile visibility.

2. Visitors Know Immediately

Users have developed an acute sensitivity to generic AI writing — even if they can't articulate it. When someone lands on your homepage and reads three sentences of corporate non-speak, they leave. Not because they identified the AI, but because nothing felt real.

Trust is built through specificity. "We've helped 500+ clients in East London cut their missed call rate to zero" is specific. "We help businesses achieve their full potential" is a phrase that could apply to a therapist, a gym, an accountancy firm, or a sandwich shop. It conveys nothing.

3. It Creates a Conversion Black Hole

Every page on your website exists to move someone from curiosity to commitment. Generic AI content fails at this because it never earns trust, never answers the specific question the visitor actually has, and never provides the social proof or specificity that moves someone to book, enquire, or buy.

You can spend thousands on traffic through paid ads or SEO. If the page they land on reads like every other page on the internet, that money is wasted.

The Irony: AI CAN Be Transformative For Your Content

Here's what most people get wrong about this. The problem isn't AI. The problem is using AI as a replacement for expertise and judgement, rather than as an amplifier of it.

AI is extraordinarily good at certain things: structuring arguments, writing clearly at scale, formatting consistently, adapting tone, and removing friction from the writing process. What it cannot do is invent genuine expertise it doesn't have, generate real case studies from real clients, or know the specific nuance of your local market.

The formula that works: You provide the expertise, the specific facts, the real client outcomes, and the genuine differentiation. AI helps you express it more clearly, structure it better, and produce it faster. The human comes first. The AI amplifies.

What Good AI-Assisted Content Looks Like

Good AI-assisted content has these characteristics:

  • Specific data points — real numbers from real situations, not "many businesses report..."
  • Named examples — case studies, client names (with permission), specific outcomes
  • Local context — references to your actual market, area, and customer type
  • A clear point of view — an opinion or insight that could only come from someone who's done this work
  • Useful, not just informative — content that gives the reader something they can actually use

The Takeaways

  • AI slop is content that could describe any business, anywhere, in any industry
  • Google's algorithms are now specifically designed to identify and suppress it
  • Visitors sense generic content immediately — even if they can't articulate why — and leave
  • The solution isn't to avoid AI — it's to ensure your genuine expertise leads the process
  • Specificity (real numbers, real names, real outcomes) is the antidote to AI slop

The businesses winning online right now aren't the ones who avoided AI or the ones who delegated their entire content strategy to it. They're the ones who figured out how to use it properly. That distinction is becoming the single biggest differentiator in digital marketing — and the gap is only going to widen.
